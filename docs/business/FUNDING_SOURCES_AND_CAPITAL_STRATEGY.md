# Funding Sources and Capital Strategy for QuantFlow

Date: 2026-06-18
Status: Strategic planning document
Audience: Founders, finance, partnerships, and investor-relations teams

## 1. Objective

Identify practical funding sources and capital strategies to accelerate QuantFlow from MVP to an investor-service business while preserving runway and governance discipline.

## 2. Funding Strategy Principles

1. Prioritize non-dilutive and low-friction funding in early hardening phase.
2. Use milestone-gated fundraising tied to reliability and monetization proof.
3. Avoid debt products that require performance claims or risk-taking behavior inconsistent with compliance posture.
4. Keep treasury and lending decisions separate from user investment outcomes.

## 3. Capital Source Categories

## 3.1 Founder and Internal Capital
- Founder contributions and retained earnings from early subscriptions.
- Best used for sprint-level execution: billing, entitlements, and reliability controls.

Pros:
- Fast access, no external process.
Cons:
- Runway concentration risk.

## 3.2 Friends, Angels, and Strategic Seed Checks
- Early high-conviction investors familiar with fintech and regulated markets.
- Ideal for pre-scale commercialization and legal-compliance setup.

Pros:
- Flexible terms if aligned.
Cons:
- Dilution and governance overhead.

## 3.3 Accelerator and Grant Programs
- Fintech accelerators, innovation grants, and regional entrepreneurship programs.
- Particularly useful for productization and compliance enablement spend.

Pros:
- Non-dilutive or low-dilution in some programs.
Cons:
- Application cycles and uncertain acceptance.

## 3.4 Revenue-Based Financing (RBF)
- Financing repaid as a percent of monthly revenue.
- Suitable after recurring revenue becomes predictable.

Pros:
- Less dilution than equity.
Cons:
- Can strain cashflow if growth underperforms.

## 3.5 Venture Debt or Working Capital Lines
- Use only after recurring revenue quality and reporting discipline are proven.

Pros:
- Extends runway without immediate equity dilution.
Cons:
- Covenants and repayment risk.

## 3.6 Broker/Trading-Adjacent Capital Paths (Context)
These are often discussed by trading users but are generally not direct funding for a SaaS operating company:
- Margin loans and securities-backed credit lines (for account holders)
- Prop trading firm evaluation/funding models
- Broker-linked leverage products

Important:
- These are typically user-trading capital tools, not company-building capital.
- QuantFlow should treat them as ecosystem context, not core company funding.

## 4. Services and Pathways Related to Trading Investor Funding

If QuantFlow later adds educational content on capital pathways for users, include categories (not endorsements):
- Prop evaluation firms that fund trader accounts after rule-based assessment.
- Securities-backed lending platforms and broker credit lines.
- Specialty fintech lenders offering portfolio-secured borrowing.

Controls for any mention in product/docs:
- Provide neutral, disclosure-driven descriptions only.
- Avoid steering users toward leverage decisions.
- Include jurisdiction and suitability disclaimers.
- Require legal review before publishing partner language.

## 5. Recommended Capital Strategy by Stage

## Stage A: 0-6 Months (Now)
Primary goals:
- Paid beta launch readiness.
- Billing and entitlements in production.
- Compliance and trust controls.

Capital mix recommendation:
- Founder/internal capital + selective angel support.
- Target small bridge for execution milestones.

## Stage B: 6-12 Months
Primary goals:
- Validate retention and monetization quality.
- Expand channels and partner pilots.

Capital mix recommendation:
- Seed raise based on KPI proof.
- Optional RBF only if recurring revenue quality is stable.

## Stage C: 12-24 Months
Primary goals:
- Scale distribution and enterprise-grade capabilities.
- Expand regulated-market readiness.

Capital mix recommendation:
- Institutional seed/Series A pathway.
- Working capital tools for predictable growth functions.

## 6. Fundraising Readiness Package

Before major raise, prepare:
- Board-ready business plan with quantified assumptions.
- KPI dashboard snapshots (commercial, reliability, quant quality).
- Legal and compliance status memo.
- Incident and risk-control operating evidence.
- Product roadmap with gate completion proof.

## 7. Milestone-Linked Funding Triggers

Raise Trigger T1:
- Stripe billing + entitlement automation live.
- Paid onboarding path under 60 seconds median.

Raise Trigger T2:
- 90-day paid beta with acceptable churn and reliability.
- Strategy governance and audit trails proven.

Raise Trigger T3:
- Documented growth efficiency and repeatable channels.

## 8. Solidarity Fund Funding Relationship (Future)

The solidarity fund is not a startup financing source. It is a future, user opt-in impact allocation mechanism.

Definition:
- Users may choose to route a configurable percentage of net growth profits into vetted philanthropic pipelines.

Guardrails:
- Must remain optional and user-controlled.
- Requires legal/accounting/tax framework before implementation.
- Requires transparent ledgering and periodic impact reporting.

## 9. Risk and Governance Considerations

Key risks:
- Confusing company financing with user trading financing.
- Introducing debt before subscription quality is stable.
- Misstating third-party funding services without legal review.

Mitigation:
- Maintain clear internal policy on financing categories.
- Gate capital decisions through board and finance review.
- Use standardized disclosure language in user-facing materials.

## 11. Conclusion

QuantFlow should fund growth through milestone-driven, governance-aware capital choices:
- Early execution: internal + selective angel support.
- Early scale: seed with KPI evidence.
- Expansion: structured debt or RBF only when cashflow quality supports it.

Trading-investor loan and funded-account services are relevant as ecosystem context, but they should not be treated as primary company financing.
